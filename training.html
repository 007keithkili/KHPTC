How and where to train at KHPTC

An elite centre for learning and sports excellence – Opened in 2002 and located on several hundred acres of farmland owned by Kipchoge Keino, the centre is listed as a High Performance Training Center by the IAAF and focuses on developing middle and long distance track champions.

Our team, along with local coordinators, are on hand to guide you. They'll take you on a tour of the training roads and act as your guides throughout your stay. Trainees have complete freedom; sessions (track, fartlek, long runs) are often done together using matatu transport, but you can also run alone or with local athletes.

The centre also hosts refugee athlete scholarship holders and national teams, creating a unique, supportive environment. No Human is Limited.

Typical Weekly Training Structure

Most athletes follow this rhythm: intense Tuesdays & Thursdays, long run on the weekend, easy jogging in between.

MondayEasy jogging / recovery
TuesdayShort VMA: 15×400m or 1min fast/1min slow
WednesdayEasy jogging
ThursdayLong VMA: 6‑8×1000m or 3min fast/1min slow
FridayEasy jogging / rest
SaturdayLong outing – Moiben Road (matatu support)
SundayRest, sightseeing or easy jog

"Join the local fartleks on Tuesdays and Thursdays, or use the track at Kipchoge Keino Sports Complex in Eldoret."

On‑Site Gym & Strength Facilities

At KHPTC we combine altitude running with a fully equipped performance gym – everything you need to build power, prevent injuries and recover.

Olympic Lifting Zone

3 platforms, 8 power racks, bumper plates up to 25kg, and dedicated coaching area.

Altitude Cardio Deck

Treadmills, assault bikes, rowers, and ski ergs – all calibrated for 2,400m elevation.

Mobility & Recovery Studio

Foam rollers, massage guns, stretching cages, and physio‑led mobility sessions.

Functional & Core Area

Kettlebells, battle ropes, plyo boxes, and TRX – designed for runners and triathletes.

Environment & Altitude

The centre is located on Kazi Mingi Farm at an altitude of approximately 2,400m, providing ideal high‑altitude training conditions. The climate is warm and moderate, with average temperatures around 17–18°C, and rain possible throughout the year, usually in the late afternoon.

"The Kip Keino HPTC is open all year round and welcomes everyone – from Olympic hopefuls to leisure runners, refugee scholarship holders, and national teams. There are no fixed dates: you choose the dates that suit you."
