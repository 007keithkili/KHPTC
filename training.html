A Day in the Life

How a typical training day unfolds at KHPTC — from first light to final recovery

5:45 AM

Wake & Hydrate

Athletes rise before dawn, hydrate, and check the day's session plan posted in the dining hall.

6:30 AM

Morning Track Session

Intervals, tempo runs, or hill repeats on the track and red-dirt trails surrounding Kazi Mingi Farm.

8:30 AM

Breakfast & Recovery Fuel

A balanced meal prepared with fresh local ingredients — designed to replenish glycogen and support recovery.

10:00 AM

Rest & Nap Window

Structured rest — one of the most overlooked parts of elite training. Athletes nap, stretch, or read.

4:00 PM

Gym & Strength Work

Afternoon sessions in the gym: strength, mobility, core, or functional work depending on the training block.

6:00 PM

Recovery & Physio

Sauna, ice bath, physiotherapy, or foam rolling — all under the guidance of the recovery team.

7:30 PM

Dinner & Debrief

Evening meal with coaches and teammates — reviewing the day's data, video, and how the body feels.

9:30 PM

Lights Out

Sleep is training. Quiet hours begin, phones down, and the next day's session begins to take shape.

Why Train at 2,176 Metres?

Thin air. More red blood cells. A bigger engine — if you train smart.

2,176m Above Sea Level

The Science of Altitude

At 2,176 metres, oxygen availability drops by roughly 20%. Your body responds by producing more red blood cells and increasing capillary density — changes that translate to a bigger aerobic engine when you return to sea level.

More Red Blood Cells

Lower oxygen triggers EPO release, boosting your blood's oxygen-carrying capacity.

Lung Efficiency

Breathing in thin air strengthens respiratory muscles and improves oxygen uptake.

Cardiac Output

Your heart pumps harder at altitude — a natural conditioning stimulus.

Speed at Sea Level

The adaptation shows up on race day — especially in the final kilometres.

It's Not Just the Altitude — It's the People

Coaches, physios, nutritionists, and mentors — all in one place.

Your Coaching Team

Every athlete at KHPTC works with a coordinated support team — not a single coach. Each role has a specific purpose in your development.

Head Coach

Sets the training block, adjusts weekly load, and reviews performance data.

Strength & Conditioning Coach

Builds gym sessions that complement — not compete with — your running.

Physiotherapist

Manages injury prevention, recovery, and hands-on treatment between sessions.

Nutritionist

Designs meal plans and fueling strategies around training load and body composition.

Mentor / Sports Psychologist

Helps athletes build focus, resilience, and race-day composure.
